# EU AI Act — Enforcement Tracker

**Last reviewed**: June 2025
**Source**: EU AI Office, national competent authority announcements, EUR-Lex

> This document tracks the enforcement landscape across EU member states. The EU AI Act is a living regulation — enforcement structures are still being established. This tracker will need updating as national authorities are formally designated.

---

## How Enforcement Works

The EU AI Act uses a **two-tier enforcement structure**:

### Tier 1 — EU Level (EU AI Office)
- Oversees GPAI models and systemic risk AI
- Can investigate, sanction, and fine GPAI providers directly
- Coordinates national authorities
- Issues guidance, codes of practice, and standards
- Based in Brussels, part of the European Commission

### Tier 2 — National Level (National Competent Authorities)
- Oversee High Risk AI systems in their jurisdiction
- Handle complaints from individuals and organisations
- Can order market withdrawal, suspension, or modification
- Issue fines for non-compliance
- Each member state must designate at least one authority

---

## Fine Structure (Article 99)

| Violation | Maximum Fine |
|-----------|-------------|
| Prohibited AI practices (Article 5) | €35,000,000 or **7%** of global annual turnover |
| Other High Risk obligations | €15,000,000 or **3%** of global annual turnover |
| Providing incorrect/misleading information to authorities | €7,500,000 or **1%** of global annual turnover |
| SMEs and startups | Lower cap applies — whichever is lower (amount or % of turnover) |

> **Note**: These are maximums. Actual fines depend on severity, duration, cooperation, and whether the provider is a first-time offender.

## National Authority Status by Country

### 🟢 Designated / Operational

**Germany**
- Authority: Federal Network Agency (Bundesnetzagentur) — lead authority
- Supporting: Federal Office for Information Security (BSI) for cybersecurity aspects
- Status: Formally designated, operational framework in place
- Notable: Germany has historically been the most active EU member state in digital regulation enforcement

**France**
- Authority: CNIL (Commission Nationale de l'Informatique et des Libertés) — AI and data protection overlap handled jointly with a new AI authority
- Status: Operational, with CNIL already active in AI-related GDPR enforcement
- Notable: France is home to Mistral AI, one of Europe's leading GPAI providers — enforcement approach watched closely by industry

**Ireland**
- Authority: AI Advisory Council + Data Protection Commission (DPC) for overlapping cases
- Status: Operational — Ireland is the EU home of many US tech companies (Google, Meta, Apple) making it a critical enforcement jurisdiction
- Notable: DPC's GDPR enforcement record suggests Ireland will be a significant jurisdiction for AI Act cases involving US tech companies

**Netherlands**
- Authority: Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens (AP) — expanded mandate to cover AI
- Status: Operational
- Notable: Netherlands is home to ASML and a significant AI research ecosystem

**Spain**
- Authority: AESIA (Agencia Española de Supervisión de la Inteligencia Artificial)
- Status: Operational — Spain was among the first EU states to establish a dedicated AI supervisory authority
- Notable: AESIA is seen as a model for other member states building standalone AI authorities

### 🟡 Designated / Framework Being Built

**Italy**
- Authority: AGID (Agency for Digital Italy) + AGCOM (communications regulator)
- Status: Framework announced, full operationalisation ongoing
- Notable: Italy was the first country globally to temporarily ban ChatGPT (March 2023, later reversed) — signals an active regulatory posture

**Sweden**
- Authority: Swedish Post and Telecom Authority (PTS) — interim designation
- Status: Interim, formal authority pending
- Notable: Home to Spotify and a large AI startup ecosystem

**Poland**
- Authority: Office of Competition and Consumer Protection (UOKiK) — interim
- Status: Interim designation, formal structure in progress

**Denmark**
- Authority: Danish Business Authority
- Status: Interim designation

### 🔴 Not Yet Formally Designated

Several smaller EU member states are still in the process of formally designating their national competent authority. The EU AI Act required all member states to designate authorities by **August 2025**.

## What to Watch — Enforcement Signals

### 1. GPAI Code of Practice
The EU AI Office is developing a voluntary Code of Practice for GPAI providers. Major AI companies (OpenAI, Google, Anthropic, Mistral, Meta) are participating. This code will shape how the GPAI obligations are interpreted in practice — it is effectively a compliance roadmap written with industry input.

### 2. Ireland as Enforcement Hub
Given that most major US AI companies are EU-headquartered in Ireland (for tax reasons), the Irish DPC will likely handle many of the first significant enforcement cases involving GPAI and High Risk AI. The DPC's GDPR record — initially criticised for slowness, now issuing billion-euro fines — suggests it has developed capacity for complex tech enforcement.

### 3. Germany's BSI and High Risk AI
The BSI's existing mandate over critical infrastructure security positions it as a natural enforcer for High Risk AI in sectors like energy, transport, and finance. German industry groups have already engaged with BSI on AI Act interpretation.

### 4. Identity Verification and Border AI
Given that identity verification and border AI systems are explicitly listed in Annex III, the identity verification industry (including companies like Veriff, iProov, Onfido) will be subject to High Risk obligations. National border authorities deploying automated document verification will face scrutiny from their respective national competent authorities.

## Fine Comparison — EU AI Act vs GDPR

| Regulation | Maximum Fine | First Major Fine |
|-----------|-------------|-----------------|
| GDPR (since 2018) | €20M or 4% turnover | Luxembourg: €746M (Amazon, 2021) |
| EU AI Act (since 2024) | €35M or 7% turnover | No major fines yet (2025) |

The EU AI Act fines are **larger** than GDPR maximums. Given GDPR's enforcement trajectory — slow start, then escalating significantly — AI Act enforcement is expected to follow a similar pattern: limited activity in 2025–2026, ramping up from 2027 onward as authorities build capacity and case law develops.

## Practical Takeaway for Product Teams

| If you are... | Key enforcement risk |
|--------------|---------------------|
| A GPAI provider with EU users | EU AI Office directly; training data and copyright compliance scrutinised first |
| A High Risk AI system provider | National competent authority in the country of market placement |
| A deployer of High Risk AI | National authority in the country where you operate |
| An Irish-registered company | DPC likely lead authority for cross-border cases |
| Operating in Germany | BSI and Bundesnetzagentur — both active and technically capable |
